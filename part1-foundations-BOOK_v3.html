Greetings Don't Conjugate

Only which greeting you use shifts with time of day or formality.

[Time-appropriate greeting] + [optional wellbeing question]

Sbah l-khir, kidayer? — Good morning, how are you?

Common mistake: Assuming "Salam" alone is too casual for strangers — it's a safe default across nearly every context.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two neighbors greet each other in the morning.

Fatima

Sbah l-khir, a Karim!

صباح الخير، يا كريم!

Good morning, Karim!

Karim

Sbah l-khir, Fatima. Labas?

صباح الخير، فاطمة. لاباس؟

Good morning, Fatima. All good?

Fatima

Labas, l-hamdulillah. O nta?

لاباس، الحمد لله. وانتا؟

All good, thank God. And you?

Karim

L-hamdulillah. Bslama, khassni nemshi.

الحمد لله. بسلامة، خاصني نمشي.

Thank God. Goodbye, I need to go.

Fatima

Bslama, t'halla f rasek!

بسلامة، تهلا ف راسك!

Goodbye, take care!

The Hand-Over-Heart Greeting.

After shaking hands, it's common in Morocco to briefly place your right hand over your heart, signaling the greeting comes "from the heart." Not required, but a warm way to respond if someone does it to you.

PRACTICE

  1. Greet someone at 8:00 AM. At 7:00 PM.
  2. Complete: "Salam, ___?"
  3. Translate: "Goodbye, take care of yourself."

MODULE 3 · TALKING ABOUT YOURSELF

No "To Be" & the Definite Article "l-"

Essential vocabulary

ENGLISHDARIJAARABICPRONUNCIATIONEXAMPLE
IAnaانا"A-na"Ana talib. — I am a student.
student (m.) / (f.)Talib / Talibaطالب / طالبة"TA-lib" / "ta-LI-ba"Ana taliba. — I am a student. (f.)
Moroccol-Maghribالمغرب"l-MAGH-rib"Ana mn l-Maghrib. — I'm from Morocco.
the housel-darالدار"l-DAR"L-dar zwina. — The house is beautiful.
beautiful / niceZwin / Zwinaزوين / زوينة"ZWEEN" / "zwee-NA"L-joe zwin. — The weather is nice.

The No "To Be" Rule.

Darija skips "am/is/are" entirely.

Ana + [noun] = I am + [noun]

Ana talib

أنا طالب.

I am a student. (m.)

Huwa tbib

هو طبيب.

He is a doctor

Common mistake: Inserting a word for "am." "Ana talib" is already complete.

Efficient by Design.

Skipping "to be" isn't casualness — it's how the grammar works, the same way Russian or Hebrew skip a linking verb in the present tense.

PRACTICE

  1. Say "I am a student" as a man, then a woman.
  2. Add "l-" to: dar, joe.
  3. Translate: "The house is beautiful."

The Pronoun-Drop Rule.

The prefix "kan-" already tells the listener the subject is "I," so "Ana" is optional.

(Ana) + kan- + [verb] = I [verb]

Kanskun f Marrakesh. — I live in Marrakesh.

Common mistake: Keeping "Ana" every time out of English habit — not wrong, just more emphatic than natural speech.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two travelers meet for the first time at a café.

Sara

Salam! Shno smitek?

سلام! شنو سميتك؟

Hi! What's your name?

Omar

Smiti Omar. O nti, shno smitek?

سميتي عمر. وانتي، شنو سميتك؟

My name is Omar. And you?

Sara

Smiti Sara. Mnin nta?

سميتي سارة. منين نتا؟

My name is Sara. Where are you from?

Omar

Ana mn l-Maghrib, o daba kanskun f Marrakesh. O nti?

انا من المغرب، ودابا كنسكن ف مراكش. وانتي؟

I'm from Morocco, and now I live in Marrakesh. And you?

Sara

Ana mn Spania. Kanhdar 3arabiya shwiya!

انا من اسبانيا. كنهضر عربية شوية!

I'm from Spain. I speak a little Arabic!

Omar

Mtsharfin, Sara!

متشرفين، سارة!

Nice to meet you, Sara!

A quick "hi" without exchanging names and origin can feel abrupt in Morocco — the extra thirty seconds is a normal, expected part of a first meeting.

PRACTICE

  1. Introduce yourself: name, origin, residence.
  2. Drop the optional pronoun: "Ana kanskun f Casablanca."
  3. Ask a woman where she's from.

The Right-to-Left Compound Rule.

[unit] + o + [ten].

Hamsa o 3ashreen — 25 ("five and twenty")

Common mistake: English word order. Practice out loud, right-to-left.

Dirhams and Rials.

Moroccans sometimes quote prices in "rial" instead of dirham, especially in souks with older vendors. One dirham = 20 rial. If a price sounds unusually high, ask "Dirham wla rial?" (Dirham or rial?).

PRACTICE

  1. Say 1–10 aloud. 2. Form 25, 33, 22. 3. Translate: "one hundred dirhams."

Stating Your Age with "3andi".

Literally "I have [number] years."

3andi + [number] + sana

3andi tlatin sana. — I am 30 years old.

Common mistake: Building this with "Ana" + number directly ("Ana 30 sana"), copying Lesson 3.1's pattern. Age specifically uses 3andi.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two new acquaintances ask each other's age at a family gathering.

Yassine

Shhal f 3amrek?

شحال ف عمرك؟

How old are you?

Nadia

3andi sb3a o 3ashreen sana. O nta?

عندي سبعة وعشرين سنة. ونتا؟

I'm 27. And you?

Yassine

3andi tlatin sana.

عندي تلاتين سنة.

I'm 30.

PRACTICE

  1. Ask someone's age. 2. State your own. 3. Translate: "I am 25 years old."

Pattern A — countries ending in "-ya":

drop "-ya," add "-i" (m.); keep "-ya" (f./language). Almania → Almani/Almania.

Pattern B — countries ending in "-da": add "-i" (m.); add "-ia" (f./language). Finlanda → Finlandi/Finlandia.
Common mistake: Assuming every country follows one of the two patterns.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Small talk at a café between two people who just met.

Hassan

Mnin nta?

منين نتا؟

Where are you from?

Marco

Ana mn Italia. Ana Itali.

انا من ايطاليا. انا ايطالي.

I'm from Italy. I'm Italian.

Hassan

Zwin! O ana, ana Maghribi, mn l-Maghrib.

زوين! وانا، انا مغربي، من المغرب.

Nice! And me, I'm Moroccan.

Darija itself is shaped the same way as these nationality words — blending Arabic roots with French, Spanish, and Amazigh influence, at the crossroads of the Mediterranean, the Atlantic, and the Sahara.

PRACTICE

  1. Form m./f. for: Italia, Almania. 2. Why doesn't Fransa follow the -ya rule? 3. Translate: "I am Moroccan, and she is Spanish."

Standard rule — consonant-ending:

add "-a." Ustad→Ustada, Tbib→Tbiba.

Irregular rule — vowel-ending (usually "-i"): add "-ya." Boulisi→Boulisya, Muhami→Muhamya.

Common mistake: Applying "+a" to a vowel-ending word, producing an awkward double vowel.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two colleagues meet at a work event.

Amina

Shno khdemtek?

شنو خدمتك؟

What's your job?

Rachid

Ana muhami. O nti?

انا محامي. ونتي؟

I'm a lawyer. And you?

Amina

Ana ustada. Kan3llem l-3arabiya.

انا أستاذة. كنعلم العربية.

I'm a teacher. I teach Arabic.

Asking someone's profession isn't intrusive in Morocco — it's a normal, friendly way to get to know someone.

PRACTICE

  1. Feminine forms of: Ustad, Tbib, Boulisi. 2. Translate: "I am an engineer." (as a man) 3. Ask a colleague their job.

MODULE 7 · SAYING NO

The ma-...-sh Pattern

Essential vocabulary

ENGLISHDARIJAARABICPRONUNCIATIONEXAMPLE
I don't haveMa3andishماعنديش"ma-3an-DEESH"Ma3andish flous. — I don't have money.
moneyFlousفلوس"F'LOOS"3andi flous. — I have money.
not (with adjectives)Mashiماشي"MA-shee"Mashi kasoul. — Not lazy.
lazyKasoulكسول"ka-SOOL"Huwa kasoul. — He is lazy.

The ma-...-sh Sandwich.

Wrap the word in "ma-" and "-sh."

ma- + [word] + -sh = not [word]

3andi → Ma3andish — I have → I don't have

For adjectives, "mashi" goes directly before the adjective instead:

Huwa mashi kasoul. — He is not lazy.

Common mistake: Forgetting the "-sh." Both pieces are needed.

PRACTICE

  1. Negate: "3andi flous." 2. Negate: "Huwa kasoul." 3. Translate: "I don't have a car."

MODULE 8 · MONTHS & DATES

The Two Calendar Systems

Essential vocabulary

ENGLISHDARIJAARABICPRONUNCIATIONEXAMPLE
JanuaryYanayir / Shahar wahedيناير / شهر واحد"ya-na-YEER"3ayd miladi f Yanayir.
JuneYunyu / Shahar sittaيونيو / شهر ستة"YOON-yoo"Ghadi l-Maghrib f Yunyu.
todayLyoumاليوم"l'YOOM"Lyoum ghadi nemshi. — Today I'm going to go.
birthday3ayd miladiعيد ميلادي"3ayd mee-LA-dee"3ayd miladi f Mars.

Two Ways to Say the Same Month.

Formal Arabic-derived names, or everyday numbered months.

Shahar + [number] = Month [number]

Shahar wahed = Month One = January

Both are correct and widely understood.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two friends planning a trip discuss dates.

Layla

Fuqash ghadi tji l-Maghrib?

فوقاش غادي تجي المغرب؟

When are you coming to Morocco?

David

Ghadi nji f shahar tsa3, f Shutanbir.

غادي نجي ف شهر تسعة، ف شتنبر.

I'm coming in month nine, in September.

Layla

Zwin! 3ayd miladi ana f had shahar!

زوين! عيد ميلادي انا ف هاد الشهر!

Nice! My birthday is this month!

Yennayer vs. Yanayir — Two Different Things.

These look and sound almost identical but differ. Yanayir is the Arabic-derived name for the calendar month of January. Yennayer is the name of the first month of the separate Amazigh (Berber) agricultural calendar, and the Amazigh New Year celebration observed in mid-January with family gatherings and a traditional couscous meal. Since 2023, Yennayer has been an official public holiday in Morocco. Both words trace back to the Latin "Ianuarius," which is exactly why they're easy to mix up.

PRACTICE

  1. Say your birth month, both systems. 2. Translate: "I'm going to Morocco in June." 3. Difference between Yanayir and Yennayer?

Quick Reference

Sounds: 3 (deep throat) · gh (gargled r) · kh (raspy "loch") · q (deep k) · h (soft h)

Greetings: Ahlan · Salam · Salam 3alaykom · Sbah l-khir · Msa l-khir · Bslama · T'halla f rasek

Introducing yourself: Shno smitek? → Smiti... · Mnin nta/nti? → Ana mn... · Kanskun f... · Kanhdar...

Numbers 1–10: Wahed, Jouj, Tlata, Rb3a, Hamsa, Sitta, Sb3a, Tmenya, Ts3ud, 3ashra

Teens 11–19: Hdash, Tnash, Tlatash, Rb3atash, Khmstash, Settash, Sb3atash, Tmentash, Ts3atash

Tens: 3ashreen (20), Tlatin (30), Rbe3in (40), Hamsin (50), Sittin (60), Sbe3in (70), Tmanin (80), Ts3in (90), Mya (100)

Compound rule: [unit] + o + [ten] — right to left. "2" becomes Tnayn in compounds.

Age: Shhal f 3amrek? → 3andi [number] sana

Nationality patterns: -ya rule (Almania→Almani/Almania) · -da rule (Finlanda→Finlandi/Finlandia). Exceptions: Fransa→Fransawi, Masr→Masri, l-Maghrib→Maghribi, Amerika→Amriki, Spania→Spanyoli

Professions: consonant-ending +a (Ustad→Ustada) · vowel-ending +ya (Boulisi→Boulisya)

Negation: ma- + [word] + -sh (Ma3andish) · mashi + adjective (mashi kasoul)

Definite article: l- + noun (l-dar, l-Maghrib)

Months: Yanayir (Jan) ... Dujanbir (Dec), or Shahar + number · Lyoum = today
