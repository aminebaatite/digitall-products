MODULE 1 · AT THE MOROCCAN CAFÉ

At The Moroccan Café

The café is a central hub of Moroccan social life — a place to meet friends, watch a match, or just sit and observe the street for hours. Ordering confidently, and knowing your tea and coffee options, goes a long way toward being treated like a regular rather than a tourist.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Order the two signature Moroccan coffee styles

Order tea by base and herb preference

Specify exactly how much sugar you want

Ordering With "Wahed" + Noun

To order one of something, place "wahed" (one) before the item, the same pattern you already know from Part 1's numbers.

Wahed + [drink]

Wahed nus-nus. — One nus-nus.

Wahed qahwa khla. — One black coffee.

Common mistake: Forgetting that sugar preferences (bla/nqas/zayd sukkar) come as a separate phrase after the order, not folded into the noun itself.

Gibli wahed nus-nus, 3afak. — Bring me one nus-nus, please.

Bghit atay bel-luiza, nqas sukkar. — I'd like verbena tea, less sugar.

Bred d l-atay b n-n3na3, zayd sukkar. — A teapot of mint tea, extra sweet.

The Café as a Social Institution

A Moroccan café isn't just somewhere to grab a quick drink — people sit for hours, watch football matches, read the news, or simply observe the street. Ordering a drink doesn't come with an expectation to leave quickly, and lingering is completely normal.

PRACTICE

  1. Order a black coffee, please.
  2. Ask for mint tea with extra sugar.
  3. Translate: "A teapot of verbena tea, less sugar, please."
Answer key   Wahed Qahwa Khla, 3afak. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Atay b n-n3na3, zayd sukkar, 3afak. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Bred d l-atay bel-luiza, nqas sukkar, 3afak.

MODULE 2 · THE MOROCCAN FAMILY TREE

The Moroccan Family Tree

Family (l-3ayla) is central to Moroccan life, and Darija makes a distinction English doesn't: separate words for maternal and paternal relatives. Getting this right matters for actually understanding who's who in a conversation.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Name immediate family members and grandparents

Distinguish maternal from paternal aunts, uncles, and cousins

Ask about someone else's family

Maternal vs. Paternal — Not Optional Detail

Unlike English, Darija doesn't have one generic word for "aunt" or "uncle" — you must specify which side of the family. This isn't a formality; using the wrong one changes what you're actually saying.

3ammi/3ammti = father's side · Khali/Khalti = mother's side
Common mistake: Defaulting to one term for both sides out of habit from English. There's no neutral option — pick the correct side.

Khouya kbir mnni. — My brother is older than me.

3ammi kaykhdem f Casablanca. — My (paternal) uncle works in Casablanca.

Khalti jaya ghda. — My (maternal) aunt is coming tomorrow.

Family Terms as Social Respect

Referring to someone's parents as Lwalid/Lwalida shows refined manners. In casual settings, calling an older woman "Khalti" or an older man "3ammi" — even with no blood relation — is a warm sign of community bonding, not overfamiliarity.

PRACTICE

  1. Give the masculine and feminine terms for a paternal aunt/uncle, then a maternal aunt/uncle.
  2. Translate: "My grandmother's name is L3aziza."
  3. Ask someone how many siblings they have.
Answer key   Paternal: 3ammi / 3ammti. Maternal: Khali / Khalti. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Jddati smitha L3aziza. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Shhal 3andek d l-khout?

MODULE 3 · POSSESSION & BELONGING

Possession & Belonging

Darija gives you two ways to say "my," "your," "his" — an easy modern system using the word "dial," and an older system of suffixes attached directly to the noun. You'll use both constantly, so this lesson covers them together.

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Express possession using "dial" + pronoun ending

Attach possessive suffixes directly to masculine nouns

Apply the T-transition rule to attach suffixes to feminine nouns

Essential vocabulary

Pathway 1: "dial" + Pronoun Ending

This is the easier, more modern pattern — place "dial" after the noun, with the pronoun ending attached to "dial" itself, not the noun.

[noun] + dial + [ending]

Kitab diali — my book · Tomobile dialha — her car

Pathway 2: Attached Suffixes on Masculine Nouns

For masculine nouns, suffixes attach directly and cleanly.

Dar (house) → Dari (my house), Dark (your house), Daro (his house), Darha (her house)

The T-Transition Rule for Feminine Nouns

Feminine nouns ending in a silent "a" (Kamija, Mraya) need one extra step before attaching a suffix:

Remove the trailing "a."

Insert "t" (the feminine marker).

Attach the suffix.

Kamija + i → Kamij + t + i → Kamijti (my shirt)

Kamija + ek → Kamijtek (your shirt)

Mraya + ha → Mraytha (her mirror)

Common mistake: Attaching a suffix directly to a feminine noun without the "t" (saying "Kamijai" instead of "Kamijti"). The "t" is required — it's not optional flavor, it's the feminine marker doing its job.

Dari zwina. — My house is beautiful.

Kamijtek zrqa. — Your shirt is blue.

Had l-kitab dialna. — This book is ours.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two roommates are sorting out whose belongings are whose while packing.

Yasmine

Wash hada l-kitab dialek?

واش هادا الكتاب ديالك؟

Is this book yours?

Karim

La, mashi diali. Ana ndonn dialha — dial Nadia.

لا، ماشي ديالي. أنا نظن ديالها — ديال نادية.

No, it's not mine. I think it's hers — Nadia's.

Yasmine

Ah wakha. O had l-kamija zrqa?

آه واخا. وهاد الكاميجة الزرقة؟

Ah, okay. And this blue shirt?

Karim

Hadik kamijti! Shukran li lqitiha.

هاديك كاميجتي! شكرا لي لقيتيها.

That's my shirt! Thanks for finding it.

Two Systems, One Language

Having two possessive systems isn't a contradiction — the "dial" system is newer and more flexible (it can attach to almost any noun), while attached suffixes are older and tighter, especially common with close, personal nouns like family members and body parts. You'll hear both used constantly, sometimes even by the same speaker in the same conversation.

PRACTICE

  1. Say "my car" using the "dial" system.
  2. Attach the correct suffix: Dar + his.
  3. Apply the T-transition rule: Mraya + my.
  4. Translate: "This shirt is yours (singular)."
Answer key   Tomobile diali. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Daro. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Mrayti. &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; Had l-kamija dialek. (or: Kamijtek, using the attached form)

Noun-Adjective Gender Agreement

Colors follow the noun they describe and must match its gender.

[noun] + [color, matching gender]

Warda hemra — a red rose (Warda is feminine → hemra)

Sabbat khal — black shoes (Sabbat is masculine → khal)

Common mistake: Defaulting to the masculine color form regardless of the noun's gender.

Adding Shade

Zraq ghamq — dark blue · Khdar fatah — light green

Belgha sfra. — A yellow traditional slipper.

Taxi khdar. — A green taxi.

Kamija byda. — A white shirt.

Color and National Identity.

Red and green carry particular weight in Morocco — the colors of the national flag — and appear constantly in decoration and design well beyond clothing.

PRACTICE

  2. Describe a green taxi and a pink shirt (Kamija).
  3. Translate: "A dark red rose."

Ordering by Weight.

[number] + kilo + d + [produce]. Jouj kilo d l-batata — two kilos of potatoes.

Common mistake: Dropping the connector "d" between quantity and produce.

Bghit kilo d t-tfah. — I'd like a kilo of apples.

3andkom shi dllah? — Do you have any watermelon?

Setup: A customer buys produce at a souk stall.

Customer

Fresh, Daily Shopping.

Many households shop for produce daily rather than for the week — souks are freshest and busiest in the morning, and vendors expect frequent, smaller-quantity buying.

PRACTICE

  1. Ask for two kilos of tomatoes. 2. State you're a vegetarian (as a woman). 3. Ask if the vendor has bananas.
Answer key   Bghit jouj kilo d l-matisha, 3afak. 2. Ana nibatiya. 3. 3andkom banan?

Adding/Subtracting Minutes.

Hadi + [hour] + o/qel + [fraction].

L-wahda o qsm — 1:05 · S-setta qel telt — 5:40 (twenty minutes before six)

Common mistake: Stating minutes as plain numbers instead of using the fixed fraction words.

T-tleta o rbe3. — 3:15.

S-seb3a qel rbe3. — 6:45.

T-tmenya d s-sbah. — 8:00 AM.

Realistic dialogue

Setup: Two colleagues confirm a meeting time.

Nawal

Shhal hadi f s-sa3a?

شحال هادي فالساعة؟

What time is it?

Yassir

Hadi r-rb3a o rbe3.

هادي الربعة وربع.

It's a quarter past four.

Nawal

Wakha, ghadi njiw f l-khmsa qel qsm, sah?

واخا، غادي نجيو فالخمسة قل قسم، صاح؟

Okay, we're meeting at five minutes to five, right?

Yassir

Sah, ghadi nkoun temma.

صاح، غادي نكون تما.

Right, I'll be there.

Flexible, Social Time.

While the grammar is precise, social punctuality tends to be relaxed — arriving a little after a stated time for a casual meetup is common and not considered rude.

PRACTICE

  1. Say 2:10. 2. Say "quarter to nine." 3. Add the AM marker to: T-tleta.
Answer key   J-jouj o qsmayn. 2. T-tes3ud qel rbe3. 3. T-tleta d s-sbah.
